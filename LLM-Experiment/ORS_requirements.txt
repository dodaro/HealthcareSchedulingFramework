OPERATING ROOM SCHEDULING - PROBLEM SPECIFICATION
=================================================

This document specifies a surgical scheduling problem completely. Together with
an instance, it contains everything needed to determine whether a proposed
schedule is valid and how good it is. Read it in full before producing a
schedule.


1. THE PROBLEM
--------------

A hospital operating department must plan a set of surgical requests over a
planning horizon of one or more days.

Scheduling a request means deciding, all at once, the day, the shift, the
starting time slot, the operating room, the surgeon and the anaesthetist. A
request is either scheduled completely, with all six decisions taken, or not
scheduled at all. Partial assignments are not allowed.

Not every request has to be scheduled. Requests that cannot be accommodated are
left out. The goal is to leave out as few as possible, giving precedence to the
more urgent ones.


2. CONVENTIONS
--------------

Days
  The planning horizon consists of consecutive days, numbered 1, 2, 3, and so on.

Shifts
  Each day is divided into two shifts: a morning shift and an afternoon shift.
  A surgery belongs to exactly one shift of exactly one day. A shift is always
  identified by naming its day and saying whether it is the morning or the
  afternoon one.

Time slots
  Each shift is divided into time slots of equal length. All shifts have the
  same number of slots. Slots within a shift are numbered consecutively, and the
  instance states the number of the first slot and the number of slots per
  shift. Slot numbers restart in every shift: slot 3 of the morning shift of day
  1 and slot 3 of the afternoon shift of day 1 are different moments in time.

Durations
  The duration of a surgery is a whole number of time slots.

Occupancy
  A surgery that starts at slot s and lasts d slots occupies the slots
  s, s+1, ..., s+d-1. It does not occupy slot s+d. Two surgeries overlap if
  they are in the same shift of the same day and they occupy at least one slot
  in common.

Non-preemption
  A surgery cannot be interrupted and resumed later. Once it starts it runs for
  its whole duration in the same room, with the same surgeon and the same
  anaesthetist.

Specialties
  Every surgical request belongs to one medical specialty. Operating rooms,
  surgeons and anaesthetists are qualified for specialties, and this is what
  determines which of them may be used for a request.


3. WHAT AN INSTANCE PROVIDES
----------------------------

An instance describes the following.

  a. The number of days in the horizon, the number of time slots per shift, and
     the number of the first slot of a shift.

  b. The surgical requests. For each request: an identifier, a priority level
     (1, 2 or 3, where 1 is the most urgent), the duration of the surgery in
     time slots, and its medical specialty.

  c. The opening plan of the operating rooms. For each operating room, the
     shifts and days in which it is open, and the medical specialty to which it
     is allocated in each of those shifts. A room that is not listed as open in
     a shift cannot be used in that shift.

  d. The surgeons. For each surgeon, the medical specialty they are qualified
     for, and the shifts and days in which they are on duty.

  e. The anaesthetists. For each anaesthetist, the medical specialty they are
     qualified for, and the shifts and days in which they are on duty.

  f. The daily workload limits. For each surgeon and each day, the maximum total
     surgery time, in time slots, that the surgeon may accumulate on that day.
     The same for each anaesthetist and each day. The two sets of limits are
     independent of one another.

     A daily limit covers both shifts of the day taken together, and it may be
     smaller than the length of a single shift. A surgeon who is on duty in two
     shifts of the same day therefore cannot necessarily fill both of them: the
     daily limit applies regardless of how many shifts the person is on duty
     for, and is often the binding constraint of the instance.


4. WHAT A SOLUTION MUST SPECIFY
-------------------------------

A solution is a list of scheduled requests. For each scheduled request it
states: the request identifier, the day, the shift, the starting time slot, the
operating room, the surgeon and the anaesthetist.

Requests that do not appear in the list are unscheduled. The solution must also
state explicitly which requests were left unscheduled.


5. REQUIREMENTS
---------------

A solution is valid only if all of the following hold.

R1 - One assignment per request
  Each request appears at most once in the schedule. A request cannot be
  performed twice, nor split between two rooms, two surgeons, two anaesthetists
  or two starting times.

R2 - The room must be open and of the right specialty
  A request may be assigned to an operating room only if, in that shift of that
  day, the room is open and is allocated to the medical specialty of the
  request.

R3 - The surgeon must be qualified and on duty
  A request may be assigned to a surgeon only if the surgeon is qualified for
  the medical specialty of the request and is on duty in that shift of that day.

R4 - The anaesthetist must be qualified and on duty
  A request may be assigned to an anaesthetist only if the anaesthetist is
  qualified for the medical specialty of the request and is on duty in that
  shift of that day.

R5 - The surgery must fit within the shift
  Let L be the number of slots of a shift. A surgery of duration d may start at
  slot s only if s + d does not exceed L. A surgery may not continue into the
  following shift or the following day.

  Apply this condition literally. For example, if a shift has 5 slots numbered
  1 to 5, then a surgery lasting 1 slot may start at slot 1, 2, 3 or 4, but not
  at slot 5; and a surgery lasting 3 slots may start only at slot 1 or at slot
  2. The last slot of a shift is therefore never occupied. Do not relax this
  condition even if it appears to waste the final slot: a schedule that violates
  it is invalid.

R6 - One surgery at a time in an operating room
  Two surgeries assigned to the same operating room, in the same shift of the
  same day, must not overlap.

R7 - One surgery at a time for a surgeon
  Two surgeries assigned to the same surgeon, in the same shift of the same
  day, must not overlap.

R8 - One surgery at a time for an anaesthetist
  Two surgeries assigned to the same anaesthetist, in the same shift of the same
  day, must not overlap.

R9 - Daily workload limits
  For each surgeon and each day, the sum of the durations of the surgeries
  assigned to that surgeon on that day must not exceed the daily limit of that
  surgeon for that day. The same must hold for each anaesthetist and each day.
  The sum covers both shifts of the day.

R10 - All urgent requests must be scheduled
  Every request of priority 1 must be scheduled. A schedule that leaves out even
  one request of priority 1 is not valid, regardless of anything else.


6. HOW SOLUTIONS ARE RANKED
---------------------------

Among valid solutions, the better one is determined in two steps, in this order.

  Step 1. Count how many requests of priority 2 are left unscheduled. Fewer is
          better.

  Step 2. Only if two solutions leave the same number of priority-2 requests
          unscheduled, count how many requests of priority 3 are left
          unscheduled. Fewer is better.

The first step dominates the second completely. A solution that leaves 3
priority-2 requests unscheduled and no priority-3 request is worse than a
solution that leaves 2 priority-2 requests unscheduled and 40 priority-3
requests unscheduled.

Requests of priority 1 play no part in this ranking, because R10 already
requires all of them to be scheduled.

Nothing else is measured. In particular, a solution is not preferred because it
finishes earlier in the day, uses fewer rooms, distributes the work more evenly
among surgeons or anaesthetists, leaves fewer gaps between surgeries, or groups
surgeries of the same specialty together. Two solutions that leave unscheduled
the same number of priority-2 requests and the same number of priority-3
requests are equally good, however different they look.


7. WHAT IS NOT CONSTRAINED
--------------------------

The following are deliberately not part of the problem. Do not enforce them and
do not treat them as preferences.

  - Requests have no deadline and no earliest date. A request may be scheduled
    on any day of the horizon on which the requirements above can be met.
  - There is no setup, cleaning or changeover time between two surgeries in the
    same operating room. A surgery may start in the slot immediately after the
    previous one has ended.
  - There is no preparation or recovery time around a surgery. A surgery
    occupies its room, its surgeon and its anaesthetist for exactly its
    duration, and nothing more.
  - Surgeons and anaesthetists are interchangeable within their specialty.
    Nothing requires a particular pairing of surgeon and anaesthetist, and
    nothing requires two related requests to receive the same staff or the same
    room.
  - There is no travel or transfer time between rooms.
  - Beds, wards and post-operative care are not modelled.
  - The workload limits are daily. There is no constraint that spans two or more
    days, such as a minimum rest between consecutive shifts, a bound on
    consecutive working days, or a weekly maximum.
  - No room, surgeon or anaesthetist has to be used, and none has to be kept
    free.


8. OUTPUT FORMAT
----------------

Report the solution as a table with one row per scheduled request and the
following columns, in this order:

  request, day, shift, starting slot, operating room, surgeon, anaesthetist

Use "morning" or "afternoon" in the shift column. After the table, list the
identifiers of the unscheduled requests, grouped by priority, and state the two
ranking figures: the number of unscheduled priority-2 requests and the number of
unscheduled priority-3 requests.

Do not include any other commentary inside the table.
